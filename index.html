Erasmus+ is the European Union's flagship programme supporting education, training, youth, and sport across Europe and beyond. It provides opportunities for students, staff, and young people to study, train, gain experience, or volunteer abroad, fostering personal development, intercultural understanding, and professional growth.

The programme operates across higher education, vocational education and training, school education, adult education, and youth sectors, covering both mobility projects and collaborative partnerships between institutions.

Eligibility depends on the specific action and sector you are applying under. Generally, students enrolled at a higher education institution that holds a valid Erasmus Charter for Higher Education (ECHE) are eligible to apply for a student mobility grant.

Staff members at participating institutions may also apply for teaching or training assignments abroad. Eligibility can extend to nationals of EU member states as well as participants from partner countries, subject to the rules of the specific call. Always check the eligibility criteria published by your home institution's International Relations Office.

Common requirements across most Erasmus+ student mobility actions include:

  • Being enrolled at a participating higher education institution
  • Having completed at least one academic year of study
  • Holding an inter-institutional agreement between your home and host institution
  • Meeting the minimum language proficiency level required by the host institution
  • Not exceeding the maximum mobility period allowed within your study cycle

Additional requirements such as academic performance thresholds or field-of-study criteria may be set by your institution. Contact your International Relations Office for the exact requirements applicable to you.

While exact requirements vary by institution and destination, most Erasmus+ applications involve preparing some or all of the following:

  • Completed application form (provided by your institution)
  • Current academic transcript or proof of enrollment
  • Motivation letter explaining your study goals abroad
  • Curriculum Vitae (CV) in Europass or standard format
  • Language proficiency evidence (certificate or self-assessment)
  • Learning Agreement signed by all three parties
  • Valid passport or national identity document

Some host institutions may request additional documents such as a portfolio, letters of recommendation, or health insurance details. Always verify with both your home and host institution.

The application process typically follows these steps:

  1. Research: Identify partner institutions with agreements in your field of study.
  2. Internal nomination: Apply through your home institution's International Relations Office by their internal deadline.
  3. Selection: Your home institution nominates selected candidates to the host institution.
  4. Host application: Complete any additional application required directly by the host institution.
  5. Learning Agreement: Draft and get your Learning Agreement approved before departure.
  6. Grant agreement: Sign the financial grant agreement with your home institution.
  7. Pre-departure: Arrange accommodation, visa if needed, and complete online language courses.

The Erasmus+ grant is intended as a contribution toward the additional costs of living and travelling abroad. It generally covers:

  • A monthly subsistence contribution based on the destination country
  • A travel contribution calculated according to the distance between home and host country
  • Possible top-up grants for students with fewer opportunities or with disabilities

Importantly, the grant does not usually cover the full cost of living abroad. Students are expected to supplement it with personal savings, national grants, or other funding sources. Tuition fees at the host institution are waived under Erasmus+ agreements.

Mobility periods vary depending on the type of activity:

  • Study mobility: Between 2 and 12 months per study cycle
  • Traineeship mobility: Between 2 and 12 months
  • Combined study + traineeship: Up to 24 months in a single cycle

Over the course of a full degree programme, the total Erasmus+ mobility supported per cycle is capped — typically 12 months per cycle for Bachelor's and Master's, and up to 12 months for a Doctorate. Planning your mobility early helps ensure you can use the maximum period available to you.

Selection is carried out by the home institution and must follow fair, transparent, and consistent criteria. Common evaluation factors include:

  • Academic performance (GPA or equivalent)
  • Quality and clarity of the motivation letter
  • Relevance of the proposed study plan to the candidate's degree
  • Language proficiency in the language of instruction
  • Priority considerations for first-time Erasmus participants
  • Possible interviews conducted by the selection committee

Institutions are encouraged to prioritise participants with fewer opportunities. Results are communicated in writing, and a waiting list is usually maintained in case a nominated candidate withdraws.

Starting your preparation well in advance significantly improves your chances and reduces last-minute stress. Consider the following steps before submitting your application:

  • Attend information sessions organised by your International Relations Office
  • Research partner institutions and their course catalogues in your subject area
  • Check language requirements for your target destinations
  • Enrol in or complete an online language course if needed
  • Draft a clear and personal motivation letter well ahead of the deadline
  • Update your CV and collect any supporting documents early
  • Speak with students who have previously completed an Erasmus+ mobility

Being well-prepared not only strengthens your application but also helps you make an informed choice about where and when to go.
